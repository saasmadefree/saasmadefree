## Blocking defects

- **ahrefs** · 1 (honesty) + 5 · `faq[4].a`, `faq[5].a`, `alternativeNotes.open-seo` · Blocking: **yes**.
  `faq[4].a` says "None of the four projects offers backlink data". `faq[5].a` answers "Can a free tool show me my competitors' backlinks?" with "Not with these four projects. None of them has a backlink index or a competitor database." Both are false. The OpenSEO README lists "Competitor Insights" and "Backlinks" among its main workflows (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/every-app/open-seo/HEAD/README.md). Our own project page `data/i18n/en/projects/open-seo.json` says the same thing. The note's "no backlink crawl" is literally true, but in context it reads as "no backlinks".
  Fix: say that OpenSEO shows backlink and competitor data bought per request from DataForSEO. It has no index of its own, and its coverage is DataForSEO's, not Ahrefs'. Rewrite FAQ 6 as "Yes, with OpenSEO, at DataForSEO's per-request price; the other three cannot." Change the note to "no backlink index of its own; backlink data comes from DataForSEO". The intro's "For link research on competitors, keep paying Ahrefs" can stay if it gives the reason (Ahrefs' index depth and history), not "no tool does it".

- **semrush** · 1 (honesty) + 5 · `alternativesIntro`, `faq[4].a`, `alternativeNotes.open-seo` · Blocking: **yes**.
  `faq[4].a` says "keep Semrush only if you need competitor data", and the intro says "For competitor research, keep paying." Both suggest OpenSEO has no competitor or backlink data, but its README lists both ("Competitor Insights", "Backlinks"). The note has the same "no backlink crawl" problem as on ahrefs.
  Fix: say that OpenSEO covers competitor and backlink lookups through DataForSEO at a cost per request. Keep Semrush for the depth of its database, its PPC and SERP history, and its traffic estimates. Do not say competitor data is missing.

- **chatgpt** · 3 · `alternativesIntro`, `faq[4]`, `data/tools/chatgpt.json` `alternatives[0]` · Blocking: **yes**.
  The winner, Ollama, is open to dispute. It is a model runtime with a CLI and an API. Our own note says "the chat features are basic without a separate interface". The intro has to tell the reader to "pair Ollama with Open WebUI", and Open WebUI is not OSI open source. A reader who knows this field would say the best free ChatGPT alternative should be one product with a chat window.
  Better pick: **Jan**. It is a desktop chat app under Apache 2.0 (https://jan.ai/docs/desktop: "Open source: Apache"). It downloads and runs open models offline with no account, and it can also use OpenAI or Anthropic keys. It offers an OpenAI-compatible local server, so it covers what Ollama does for one person. For a team or a shared server, LibreChat (MIT, multi-user, ChatGPT-style) is the second option to name.
  Fix: put `jan` first in `alternatives` and add a source line with `"field": "alternatives order"`. Rewrite the intro's first sentence and FAQ 5 around Jan, then mention LibreChat for teams and Ollama for developers who want an API. Or keep Ollama, but then add a sourced justification and say plainly that a second tool is needed.

- **opennutritracker** · 4 · `tagline` · Blocking: **yes**.
  "Open-source calorie, macro and micronutrient tracker for Android and iOS…" is nearly the README's first line: "Open-source calorie, macro, and micronutrient logging for Android and iOS."
  Fix: write it in our own words, for example "Phone calorie and nutrient diary with barcode scanning; no account, data stays on the device."

- **wger** · 4 · `description` · Blocking: **yes**.

  Fix: rephrase it, for example "Several people can share one server, and a trainer can manage gym members."

- **daily-value** · 1 + 4 · `description`, `limits[0]` · Blocking: **yes**.
