route 1 of 3
ALT-01

Listmonk

Full

A newsletter and mailing-list manager shipped as a single Go binary. Postgres is its only dependency.

Use when
Newsletters and one-off campaigns to large lists, on a server that costs a few dollars a month.
Limits
  • No multi-step visual automation journeys.
  • It does not deliver mail itself: you plug in SMTP or SES, and deliverability becomes your job.

Build it yourself

route 2 of 3

A contact list with tags and segments, a Markdown campaign composer, and sends through a transactional email API with compliant unsubscribe handling built in.

prompt.txt1,182 chars · any coding agent
Build a small-list email campaign tool: contacts with tags and custom fields, CSV import and export, and segments defined as saved filters (has a given tag, joined after a date, opened the last campaign). Write campaigns in Markdown, rendered into one clean single-column HTML email with an automatically generated plain-text alternative. Send through a real transactional email API (Amazon SES or similar) with throttling and a per-recipient status; wire up bounce and complaint webhooks to automatically suppress those addresses going forward. Bake compliance in rather than bolting it on: an unsubscribe link and your postal address in every footer, a List-Unsubscribe header, and double opt-in on the public signup form. Make open tracking a per-campaign toggle, off by default. Show a campaign report with sent, bounced, and unsubscribed counts.

Do not build a drag-and-drop template editor, visual automation journeys, or e-commerce integrations — those are real, separate products. Be blunt in the README: inbox placement is earned through sending behavior over time, not configured. Document SPF, DKIM, and DMARC setup, and warm up sending volume gradually on a new domain.

Why people still pay

Email is business-critical, and deliverability problems are expensive to diagnose and fix — people pay so inbox placement is someone else's ongoing responsibility.

§6

FAQ

6 questions
What’s the best free Mailchimp alternative?
For most newsletters, Listmonk. It is free, AGPL-licensed and light enough for a small server. It does not deliver mail on its own, so pair it with a sending service such as Amazon SES. If you need visual automation journeys, Mautic is the open-source option that has them, at the price of a much heavier install.
Can I move from Mailchimp to Listmonk without losing subscribers?
Yes. Export your audience from Mailchimp as CSV and import it into Listmonk, keeping unsubscribed and cleaned contacts flagged so you never mail them again. Automations and templates do not transfer. Warm up the new sending domain slowly: send to your most engaged readers first.
Can I import my existing Mailchimp audience?
Yes — Mailchimp exports your contacts, tags, and custom fields to CSV, which imports directly here. Automation journeys and templates don't transfer; that logic has to be rebuilt from scratch.
Will it work on my phone?
The admin view is a plain web page, usable on a phone for a quick check, but the composer isn't optimized for a small screen and there's no dedicated app.
What does it cost to run?
Hosting, a domain, and a database, typically a few dollars a month, plus per-email sending costs on the transactional API — often well under Mailchimp's tiered pricing at small list sizes.
What's the one thing that doesn't survive the rebuild?
Sending reputation. Mailchimp's shared and dedicated IPs carry years of established trust with inbox providers; a brand-new sending domain starts at zero and has to earn that trust the same slow way, regardless of how good the code is.
